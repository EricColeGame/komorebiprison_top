## Why Outfits Matter More Than You Think in Komorebi Prison

Learning Komorebi Prison how to change clothes is one of those small skills that turns urgent the second a guard orders you into a clean uniform before visitation. New players often assume clothing is cosmetic, then lose comfort, hygiene, or reputation because they never swapped out of a work outfit.

If you have ever searched for Komorebi Prison how to change clothes and found nothing but vague forum replies, this guide walks through the entire flow — where to go, which menu to open, what can go wrong, and how to fix it.

A quick note before we start: Komorebi Prison is actively updated, and its PC and mobile builds do not always share identical menu labels. The steps below describe the standard flow. If your build names a button differently, the underlying order of operations is the same.

### What Your Outfit Actually Affects

In prison-life games, clothing usually feeds into several overlapping systems rather than sitting in a cosmetic-only slot. Komorebi Prison follows that pattern: what you wear can influence how guards read you, which jobs you can accept, and whether certain NPCs will talk to you at all.

| System | How clothing influences it | Typical effect |
|---|---|---|
| Hygiene and comfort | Soiled or wet outfits drag down daily meters | Lower mood, slower stamina recovery |
| Guard suspicion | Certain outfits stand out in restricted zones | More frequent stop-and-search checks |
| NPC dialogue | Inmates react to faction colors and job uniforms | New dialogue options, or flat refusals |
| Job access | Work outfits are often required to clock in | Pay docked or shift cancelled |
| Yard and recreation | Weather-appropriate gear matters outdoors | Comfort penalties in rain or cold |
| Disguise mechanics | Layered outfits can hide contraband or a change of clothes | Risky, but enables escape routes |

The practical takeaway: treat your outfit as equipment, not decoration. Checking it once per in-game day prevents most of the problems players complain about.

## Komorebi Prison How to Change Clothes: Step-by-Step Walkthrough

The core loop is short once you know it. Almost every version of the game hides the same sequence behind a different-looking interface.

| Step | Action | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Move to a valid changing spot | Your cell, the shower block, or a locker room |
| 2 | Face the container and trigger the interact prompt | A wardrobe, footlocker, dresser, or locker icon |
| 3 | Open your character or inventory panel | Tabs for items, clothing, and equipped gear |
| 4 | Select the clothing or outfit tab | A paper-doll view or a grid of garments |
| 5 | Highlight the garment you want | Equipped items are usually marked or outlined |
| 6 | Confirm the equip or wear action | A prompt asking you to confirm the swap |
| 7 | Close the menu and check your status bar | Hygiene, comfort, and uniform indicators should update |

Two habits separate smooth runs from frustrating ones. First, always change in a designated spot — mid-corridor swaps are blocked by design, and attempting them can flag you for suspicious behavior. Second, confirm the swap before you walk away. In some builds the menu closes on its own and the change silently fails.

If the garment refuses to equip, it is almost always one of three causes: it is dirty, it belongs to another inmate, or it is flagged as contraband and therefore unequippable in a public area.

## Where You Can Change Clothes in Komorebi Prison

Location matters more than most guides admit. Some spots are private and safe, others are fast but visible, and a few let you swap outfits while a guard is watching — which rarely ends well.

| Location | Best for | Practical notes |
|---|---|---|
| Your cell | Routine daily swaps | Private and safe, but only during unlocked hours |
| Shower block | Post-shower changes | High traffic; items can go missing if left unattended |
| Locker room | Work and gym outfits | Usually requires an active job or scheduled activity |

Community reports suggest the locker room and laundry room are the two fastest options, while the cell remains the safest. If you are carrying anything you should not be, never change in the laundry room — that is the single most-searched area in most prison sims.

## Outfit Slots, Uniform Rules, and Laundry

Most prison simulations split clothing into discrete slots rather than one single outfit. Knowing which slot you are editing stops the "I changed clothes but nothing happened" confusion.

| Slot | What belongs there | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Uniform / work outfit | Issued prison clothing | Required during roll call and shifts |
| Casual / recreation | Yard-appropriate clothing | Affects comfort during free time |
| Sleepwear | Night clothing | Ties into rest quality |
| Job-specific gear | Kitchen, workshop, laundry aprons | Missing gear can cancel your shift |
| Outerwear | Jackets and weather gear | Reduces cold and rain penalties |
| Concealed layer | Hidden items or a spare outfit | High risk, high reward |

Laundry is the mechanic that trips up the most players. Dirty clothing usually cannot be equipped, so the loop is: remove the soiled item, place it in a hamper or washing machine, run the cycle, dry it, then collect it and re-equip. Skipping the drying step leaves you with damp clothing that still counts as unwearable.

A useful rule of thumb: keep one clean uniform set in your cell storage at all times. That single habit means you always have something to change into, even if a shift runs long or your laundry gets mixed up with another inmate's.

## Troubleshooting: When You Cannot Change Clothes in Komorebi Prison

When Komorebi Prison how to change clothes stops working the way it should, the cause is usually mechanical rather than a bug. Work through the table below before you assume the game is broken.

| Problem | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| No interact prompt appears | You are not in a valid changing zone | Walk to your cell or the locker room |
| "You cannot change here" message | Restricted area or active lockdown | Wait for the schedule to move to free time |
| Garment missing from inventory | It is dirty, confiscated, or stored elsewhere | Check your cell storage and laundry |
| Outfit reverts after reloading | The swap was not saved | Change clothes, then trigger a save or sleep |
| Guard confiscates your clothing | Item flagged as contraband | Store it in a hidden container, not on your body |
| Menu freezes mid-swap | UI hiccup, common on lower-end devices | Close and reopen the panel, or restart the session |
| New outfit has no visual effect | Cosmetic layer only | Check whether the item is a skin or a functional garment |

If a swap fails repeatedly in the same spot, try a different location entirely. Several players report that moving from the shower block to the cell resolved an interaction that simply would not register.

## FAQ: Changing Clothes in Komorebi Prison

**Can you change clothes anywhere in Komorebi Prison?**

No. Changing is restricted to designated areas such as your cell, the shower block, locker rooms, and the laundry. Attempting a swap in a corridor or restricted zone is blocked, and repeated attempts can draw guard attention.

**How do you change clothes in Komorebi Prison if the wardrobe is locked?**

Locked storage usually means you are outside the scheduled window for that area. Wait for free time or a shift change, or use an alternative spot like your cell footlocker, which is typically accessible during unlocked hours.

**Do you need to wash clothes before changing in Komorebi Prison?**

Dirty garments generally cannot be equipped. Run them through the laundry cycle and dry them fully first, otherwise they stay in an unwearable state even though they appear in your inventory.

**Does changing clothes help you escape in Komorebi Prison?**

It can help, but indirectly. Disguises and concealed layers may let you pass through areas where your standard uniform would stand out. The tradeoff is risk: contraband clothing is exactly what guards search for, so weigh the reward against the chance of losing your progress.
