## Why the Komorebi Prison Interior Rewards Careful Reading

Step through the main gate and the Komorebi Prison interior immediately tells you what kind of challenge you are in for: long wings, narrow catwalks, and light that creeps across the floor as the day cycle turns. That matters because the interior is the single biggest variable in how a run unfolds — it decides where you can hide, where you can be seen, and how quickly you can move between floors. Players who treat the Komorebi Prison interior as simple set dressing tend to burn their first ten minutes wandering. Players who read it like a map tend to control the pace of the entire session.

The short version: the interior behaves like a system, not a backdrop. Corridor width, ceiling height, and window placement all feed into three mechanics you use constantly — line of sight, sound propagation, and travel time. Adjust any one of those and the same hallway becomes a completely different decision.

| Interior zone | Signature feature | Primary mechanic | Typical traffic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Entry block | Wide doors, hard floors | Loud footstep audio | Very high early in a run |
| Cell wings | Repeating doors, narrow frames | Frequent sightline breaks | Moderate |
| Central corridor | Long, straight, tall windows | Long-range visibility | High during rotations |
| Workshop and laundry | Machinery, soft surfaces | Sound masking | Low to moderate |
| Upper catwalks | Open railings, grated floors | Vertical sightlines | Low |
| Yard-facing galleries | Large glazing, direct light | Timing windows | Spikes with the light cycle |

Community reports consistently describe the entry block as the loudest space in the complex. Hard flooring and tall ceilings give footsteps nowhere to go, so a sprint through the gate can be heard well before you are visible. Walking the first thirty feet instead of sprinting it is one of the cheapest habits you can build, and it costs you almost nothing in time.

## Mapping the Wing Layout: Cells, Corridors, and Control Points

The Komorebi Prison interior is organized around a spine — a long central corridor with cell wings branching off at regular intervals. Once you notice that rhythm, the whole floor plan becomes predictable, and predictability is what lets you plan two moves ahead instead of reacting to whatever is in front of you.

### Reading the grid

Each wing tends to follow the same template: a narrow entry, a row of cells on one side, and a service alcove at the far end. That alcove is usually the reason the wing exists at all. Learning the template means you only have to learn a wing once — every other wing is a variation on a shape you already know.

### Control points

Control points are the doorways, stair heads, and junction corners that let one person watch two or more routes at the same time. They are the highest-value real estate inside the Komorebi Prison interior, and they are almost never in the middle of a room. Corners and thresholds do the work; open floor does not.

| Control point | Watches | Approach angle | Value |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wing junction corner | Two wings plus spine | Side-on | Very high |
| Stair head landing | Stairs, catwalk, corridor | From above | High |
| Laundry doorway | Workshop and rear hall | Oblique | Moderate |
| Gallery end window | Yard route and gallery | Long | Situational |
| Cell block cross-aisle | Both cell rows | Straight | High in the first minutes |

A useful exercise for new players: walk one full wing without touching anything, then name every point where you could see two routes at once. Most people find three or four. Those are the spots worth memorizing.

## Light and Time: How the Interior Changes Across a Session

Lighting is the mechanic that separates a good run from a great one. Because the complex is built around open galleries and tall windows, the light state shifts as time passes, and visibility shifts with it. A route that feels safe at one point can be wide open a few minutes later.

| Light state | What you can see | What others see | Best use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Direct | Long, hard shadows | You are backlit near windows | Cross open spaces quickly |
| Dappled | Patchy, broken shapes | Silhouettes blur at distance | Move while shapes are noisy |
| Diffuse | Even, flat light | Detail is easy to read | Do careful, precise work |
| Low | Deep shadow in corners | Movement reads better than shape | Hold position, observe |
| Artificial | Pools of light at fixtures | Fixtures mark your position | Use the dark gaps between pools |

The practical rule is simple: never cross a lit gallery on a timer you did not set yourself. Wait for the transition, move on the change, and you halve the number of eyes that can pick you out.

### Sound is the second clock

Soft surfaces matter as much as light. Workshops, laundries, and textile storage deaden footsteps, while the spine and the cell wings amplify them. Player experience suggests that routes threaded through soft-surface rooms produce far fewer early detections than routes that simply take the shortest path. Noise is not a side effect of movement — it is a resource you spend.

## Verticality: Stairs, Catwalks, and Service Routes

The single most underrated part of the Komorebi Prison interior is how much of it happens above eye level. Grated catwalks, service ladders, and stacked landings mean that "up" is often a legitimate route rather than just a shortcut.

| Route type | Speed | Noise | Exposure | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Main stairwell | Fast | High | High at the head | Resetting after a mistake |
| Service ladder | Slow | Low | Low if timed | Entering a wing unseen |
| Catwalk span | Moderate | Moderate (grated) | High from below | Watching two wings at once |
| Laundry chute | Very fast, one-way | Moderate | None once inside | Emergency exits |
| Exterior gallery | Moderate | Low | High near glazing | Moving between wings |

Grated catwalks deserve a warning. They are quiet when you stand still and surprisingly loud when you move, and anyone below can look up and read your position through the mesh. Treat a catwalk as a viewing platform, not a highway.

### Use height to gain information, not distance

The best reason to climb is to see the next two minutes before they happen. Descending early, while you still know where everyone is, beats descending late when the information is stale. If you cannot name what you learned from a catwalk, you probably should not have taken it.

## High-Value Rooms, Risks, and Common Mistakes

Not every room in the complex is worth your time. Priority depends on what your run needs, but the ranking below holds up across most sessions.

| Room | What it offers | Risk level | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Workshop | Tools, cover, sound masking | Moderate | High |
| Laundry | Soft surfaces, chute access | Low | High |
| Control office | Overwatch position, records | High | Moderate |
| Infirmary | Supplies, quiet corner | Low | Moderate |
| Cell block | Many exits, many eyes | High | Low unless you need a cell |
| Storage annex | Dead ends and clutter | Low | Situational |

### Mistakes that get players caught

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Better habit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sprinting the entry block | Audio carries down the whole spine | Walk the first stretch |
| Crossing galleries on a fixed count | Light transitions rarely match your count | Move on the change, not the number |
| Using catwalks as main routes | Grated floors broadcast every step | Watch from them, then descend |
| Ignoring service alcoves | They hide the fastest exits | Check the far end of every wing |
| Backtracking through lit corridors | You re-enter a space people are watching | Loop forward instead of reversing |

Player experience suggests the biggest gains come from the least dramatic changes: walking instead of sprinting, waiting one light cycle, and checking one extra alcove per wing. None of that requires better gear or faster reflexes.

### A quick pre-run checklist

- Identify the nearest soft-surface room before you move.
- Pick two control points and route through at least one of them.
- Note the current light state and the next transition.
- Confirm one one-way exit — a chute, a ladder, or a service door.
- Decide in advance which wing you will skip entirely.

## FAQ

**What is the most important mechanic in the Komorebi Prison interior?**
Light. It changes what you can see, what others can see, and when a route is genuinely safe. Sound is a close second, because soft and hard surfaces behave so differently that they effectively create two separate maps.

**Should I use the catwalks or the main stairwells?**
Use the catwalks to observe and the stairwells to move. Grated floors make catwalks loud underfoot, and anyone below can track you through the mesh.

**How long does a typical run through the Komorebi Prison interior take?**
It varies by route and by how often you stop to observe. Community reports suggest most runs are effectively decided in the first few minutes, which is exactly why the entry block matters so much.

**Is it better to learn every wing or master two?**
Master two wings completely, then expand. The template repeats, so the second wing you learn takes a fraction of the time the first one did — and you will move through the Komorebi Prison interior with far more confidence.
