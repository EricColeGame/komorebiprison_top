Getting caught in Komorebi Prison is not the end of a run — it is a mechanic, and like every other mechanic in the game it can be learned, baited, and beaten. The Komorebi Prison police system decides when you get spotted, how hard officers chase, and what happens after the cuffs go on, which makes it the single most important system to understand before you plan any escape. Once you know how the Komorebi Prison police respond at each stage of a breakout, you stop guessing and start running routes that actually work.

This guide breaks the system down into its parts: detection, escalation, custody, and recovery. No fake numbers, no magic tricks — just the behavior patterns that show up again and again in player experience and community reports.

## How the Komorebi Prison Police Mechanic Works

The police in Komorebi Prison are not one officer with a chase script. They are a layered system: detection, escalation, and custody. Each layer has its own rules, and each one can be interrupted before it hands off to the next. Understanding that handoff is the entire game.

Most new players treat officers like a wall — something you either get past or you don't. Experienced players treat them like a clock. Every officer who notices you starts a timer, and your job is to be somewhere else before that timer runs out. If you can break the chain between layers, you never reach the custody layer at all.

### The Three Stages of a Police Response

| Stage | What Triggers It | What Officers Do | Player Counter |
|---|---|---|---|
| Suspicion | Restricted zone, lingering after curfew, crouching near a guard post | Turn toward you, take a few steps, call out | Break line of sight, walk back to a public area, act normal |
| Pursuit | Witnessed contraband, an assault, an open cell, a fence breach | Sprint, shout, and coordinate with nearby units | Cut corners, use doors, split away from teammates |
| Lockdown | Escape alarm, weapon drawn, officers down | Patrol density rises, gates seal, search teams sweep rooms | Hide and wait out the timer, or commit fully to the breakout |

The important detail is that these stages do not reset instantly. Dropping out of sight ends *pursuit*, but the officers who saw you stay alert for a while afterward. That lingering alertness is what catches most players — they hide successfully, then walk straight back into the same hallway thirty seconds later.

## Wanted Levels and Heat: Reading the Meter

Komorebi Prison police operate on an escalation model that most players call "heat." It is not a visible bar in every build, but the behavior is consistent enough to read. The higher your heat, the more officers are actively looking for you, and the less forgiving their detection becomes.

| Heat Tier | Typical Behavior | Does It Decay? | Best Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clear | No officer is tracking you | — | Farm, craft, scout freely |
| Warm | One officer watching, patrols slightly wider | Yes, slowly | Walk, never sprint, avoid restricted zones |
| Hot | A squad is actively hunting your last known area | Slowly, and only out of sight | Break line of sight, change floors, drop contraband |
| Locked Down | Every officer is searching; exits are watched | No, until the alarm resolves | Hide completely or commit to the escape |

A practical rule that holds up across most sessions: heat decays based on *time out of sight*, not distance traveled. Running across the map while visible does nothing for you. Sitting still behind a wall for the same amount of time does everything.

Exact decay values are tuned differently by server hosts and game versions, so treat the table above as behavior rather than math. If you want to learn your server's specific timing, run the same route twice — once hiding immediately, once running — and compare how quickly officers return to normal patrols.

## Arrest, Bail, and the Jail Loop

Getting caught in Komorebi Prison is a graded outcome, not a binary one. What you lose depends on where you were caught and what you were carrying when it happened.

| Outcome | Trigger | What You Lose | How to Recover |
|---|---|---|---|
| Escort back | Minor trespass, no contraband | Nothing | Walk back and try a different route |
| Standard arrest | Caught in a restricted area with contraband | Carried items | Re-craft from your stash |
| Full lockup | Escape attempt, weapons, or assault | Most of your inventory, longer timer | Team can smuggle or break you out |
| Maximum security | Repeat offenses in one session | Everything carried, longest timer | Wait it out, then plan a different approach |

### Why the Jail Timer Is Not Really a Punishment

Community reports consistently describe lockup as a *reset window* rather than a loss. Your heat clears, officers return to normal patrols, and the map's attention moves elsewhere. Teams that use that window well will send a free player to stash gear near the exit while the caught player waits out the timer.

The counterintuitive tip: if you are going to get caught anyway, get caught *early and quietly*. A clean arrest costs far less than a chase that ends in a lockdown, and a lockdown is much harder for your teammates to work around.

## Line of Sight, Cover, and the Rules Officers Follow

Komorebi Prison police do not have perfect awareness. They detect what they can see, and what they can see is governed by fairly consistent rules.

| Cover Type | Blocks Sight? | Blocks Chase? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solid wall or closed door | Yes | Yes | Best option; door state matters more than you think |
| Low crate or counter | Partially | No | Crouch behind it and move when the patrol turns |
| Dark corner | Partially | No | Only reliable before officers are alerted |
| Crowd or common area | No | Soft | Officers rarely chase into public rooms without cause |

Three rules that matter more than any single hiding spot:

- **Sprinting is a beacon.** Movement speed draws attention at range. Walk when you are anywhere near a patrol.
- **Doors reset pursuit, not suspicion.** Closing a door breaks the chase, but the officer still knows roughly where you went.
- **Vertical movement beats horizontal movement.** Changing floors breaks tracking far more reliably than running down a long hallway.

The design lineage here is worth knowing if you want to predict behavior. Tiered police escalation — suspicion, pursuit, then a hard lockdown — is a convention popularized by open-world crime games, and the [wanted-level systems in the Grand Theft Auto series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Theft_Auto_(series)) are the clearest documented example of how those tiers are usually tuned.

## Team Roles: Beating the Komorebi Prison Police With Squad Play

Solo escapes are possible, but the Komorebi Prison police system is designed to punish lone runners. A coordinated squad splits the system's attention, and attention is the resource you are actually fighting over.

| Role | Job | Key Skill |
|---|---|---|
| Scout | Maps patrol timing and camera coverage | Patience; never carries contraband |
| Runner | Moves tools and keys to the breach point | Route memory |
| Distractor | Deliberately pulls officers to the far side of the map | Accepting a lockup on purpose |
| Smuggler | Moves items through checkpoints in small loads | Inventory discipline |
| Lookout | Watches heat tiers and calls the moment to move | Communication |

The distractor role is the one most groups skip, and it is the one that wins runs. One player who is willing to eat a standard arrest can open a two-minute window for everyone else — which is usually more than enough time to clear a fence.

## Common Mistakes That Get Players Caught

- **Treating "out of sight" as "safe."** Officers stay alert after losing you. Wait for patrols to genuinely reset.
- **Carrying everything at once.** If a single arrest can end your run, you are carrying too much.
- **Running the same route twice.** Repeated breaches are exactly what raises patrol density.
- **Fighting officers.** It converts a standard arrest into a lockdown and usually a maximum-security outcome.
- **Ignoring the alarm timer.** Lockdowns resolve on a clock; hiding for the full duration is often faster than trying to sneak out mid-search.
- **Leaving doors open behind you.** An open restricted door is a free invitation for a patrol to check the room.
- **Splitting up without a rally point.** Regrouping after a chase is a mechanic of its own, and it needs a plan.

## Komorebi Prison Police FAQ

**What actually triggers the Komorebi Prison police to chase you?**
Witnessed rule-breaking, not proximity. Standing near an officer is fine; being seen with contraband, in a restricted zone, or near an open breach is not. Detection is about what an officer can see at that moment.

**Can you outrun the police once a pursuit starts?**
Usually not in a straight line. What works is breaking line of sight — corners, doors, stairwells, and floor changes. Officers converge on your last known position, so distance matters less than disappearing.

**How do you lose heat the fastest?**
Get out of sight and stay there. Heat decays with time out of sight, so hiding in a dead-end room for a short while beats sprinting across the map while visible.

**Is it ever better to just get arrested?**
Yes. A quiet early arrest costs little and clears your heat, while a failed chase can trigger a lockdown that traps your whole squad. Deliberate arrests are a legitimate tactic, not a failure.
