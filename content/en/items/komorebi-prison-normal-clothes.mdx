## What Are Normal Clothes in Komorebi Prison?

The fastest way off the yard isn't a tunnel under the fence — it's a change of outfit. Komorebi Prison normal clothes are the civilian garments your character can wear instead of the standard inmate uniform, and they are one of the most misunderstood items on the map. New players treat Komorebi Prison normal clothes as pure cosmetics. Veterans treat them as a tool.

That distinction matters because of how the game reads your character. A uniform announces "inmate" before anyone checks a name tag. A plain hoodie and jeans announce "visitor," "delivery driver," or "someone who belongs here." That split-second judgment is what opens doors — literally — in restricted parts of the map.

If you keep getting stopped in corridors you thought were safe, the problem usually isn't your route. It's your wardrobe.

Exact numbers shift between builds, but community reports are consistent about the direction: normal clothes lower your profile across the board.

## Why Civilian Outfits Matter More Than You Think

The real value of Komorebi Prison normal clothes isn't the fabric — it's the routes they unlock and the seconds they buy you.

- **Front entrances open up.** Many exits are watched by NPCs who react to appearance first and behavior second. Walking out the front door in civilian clothes is often easier than climbing a fence in a uniform.
- **You stop being a target.** Uniformed players get searched, blocked, and reported far more often than someone who looks like they're running an errand.
- **Trade value is real.** Outfits are among the most requested trade items in player-run economies, which makes them worth collecting even if you only wear two or three.
- **Fewer scripted reactions.** Guards and staff trigger fewer "unusual behavior" checks when your silhouette matches the area you're standing in.

## Common Mistakes With Komorebi Prison Normal Clothes

Even players who collect every set of Komorebi Prison normal clothes make the same handful of errors. Avoiding these will save you more time than hunting down a rarer outfit ever will.

- **Wearing civilian clothes inside the cell block.** Nothing flags you faster than looking like a visitor in a housing unit.
- **Mixing uniform and civilian pieces.** A prison-issue shirt with civilian pants reads as "escaped," not "staff."
- **Running everywhere.** Speed is the single loudest signal in the game.
- **Ignoring color.** Bright, saturated clothing stands out in almost every lighting condition.
- **Carrying nothing.** Civilians have a reason to be somewhere — a clipboard, a crate, a bag. Empty hands invite questions.
- **Never changing back.** If you're still in civilian clothes when the round tightens up, you're the only person on the map who looks out of place.

Treat the outfit as one part of a routine, not a magic switch. Players who pair the right clothes with the right route and the right pace escape far more consistently than players who rely on the outfit alone.

## FAQ

**Do Komorebi Prison normal clothes actually stop guards from spotting you?**
They don't make you invisible — they change how you're categorized. Guards and NPCs still react to behavior, so normal clothes work best when you pair them with a believable route and a normal walking pace.

**Where is the fastest place to get normal clothes?**
Based on community reports, the laundry and supply area is the easiest early source, followed by visitor lockers near the lobby. Both are reachable without a key or a trade.

**Can you keep outfits between rounds?**
That depends on how your server handles inventory and persistence. In many setups, items you've stored or traded are retained, while anything on your character at the end of a round may be lost. Check with your server's rules before assuming.

**Are some Komorebi Prison normal clothes better than others?**
Yes. Plain, muted outfits with common silhouettes are consistently the safest. Bright colors and formal wear stand out and should only be used when a specific route calls for them.

**Do you need normal clothes to escape at all?**
Not always — some routes rely on timing or teamwork instead. But for anything involving the front gate, the lobby, or the docks, a civilian outfit is usually the difference between walking out and getting sent back.
